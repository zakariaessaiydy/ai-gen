# kids-morals — a feeling, a choice, a lesson

Read `.claude/skills/make-kids/SKILL.md` first.

## The loop

**WANT → WRONG CHOICE → FEELING → KIND CHOICE → HAPPY ENDING → THE MORAL**

1. **Want** (hook): a character wants something a lot (the last cookie, to win the race, to play
   alone with the new ball).
2. **Wrong choice**: they grab/lie/rush/leave someone out — shown simply, not cruelly.
3. **Feeling**: the consequence is EMOTIONAL, not punishment: a friend is sad (`sad` close-up),
   and our hero feels it too ("Leo's tummy feels funny… that's a guilty feeling").
4. **Kind choice**: they fix it — share, say sorry, tell the truth, wait their turn.
5. **Happy ending**: it's better than before (sharing = a picnic for everyone).
6. **The moral**: one line by the narrator, shown big: "Sharing makes everyone happy." Then a
   question to the child: "When did YOU share something?"

## Rules

- Name the feelings (sad, jealous, proud, sorry) — emotional vocabulary is the real lesson.
- Nobody is "bad"; characters make mistakes and repair them. No scolding adults, no
  punishments, no scary consequences.
- Classic fables (Aesop: tortoise & hare, the lion & the mouse, the boy who cried wolf) are
  public domain — RETELL them in our own words with our characters; don't copy a modern
  book's text or a studio's version.
- Moral in ≤ 6 words, positive phrasing ("Telling the truth feels good", not "Don't lie").

## Bedtime long version (16:9, 5–10 min)

Bedroom set, narrator `af_heart` (rate -10%), slow camera, a lullaby bed from `gen_song.py`
(musicbox lead, piano, no drums, ~70 bpm), the story, the moral, then Mila yawning, "Goodnight,
friends" and the stars twinkle — the calm ending parents loop at bedtime. No Confetti here;
a soft sparkle instead.

## Short (9:16, 45–60 s)

Hook on the want (0–3 s) → wrong choice (by 15 s) → feeling (close-up, 5 s) → kind choice →
happy ending → moral PopText → "Would YOU share?" + loop.
