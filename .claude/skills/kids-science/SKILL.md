# kids-science — a big question, a simple picture

Read `.claude/skills/make-kids/SKILL.md` first.

## The loop: WHY? → GUESS → MODEL → SEE IT → RECAP → WOW

1. **WHY?** (hook, 0–3 s): Mila asks a real kid question to the camera, `wow`/`think` face.
2. **GUESS**: Leo's funny wrong idea ("The sky is blue because someone painted it!") — it's
   the misconception kids actually have; Hoot says "Good guess, Leo!" (never mocks).
3. **MODEL**: Prof. Hoot explains with ONE simple analogy + one diagram drawn on screen
   (arrows, a sun, drops) — no jargon; if a real word is taught, it's one word, shown big
   ("EVAPORATION — water goes up!").
4. **SEE IT**: the animated demonstration (water drops rising from the sea into a cloud) or a
   SAFE experiment the child can do with an adult (ice cube in the sun, a magnet and paper
   clips) — always "Ask a grown-up to help!".
5. **RECAP**: 3 icons in order (Sun → water goes up → cloud → rain) said once more.
6. **WOW fact** to end: one surprising true fact (Shorts loop back).

## Accuracy rules (science parents check)

- Simplify, never falsify. The analogy must not create a NEW misconception; say "a bit like…".
- Keep a source line per fact in script.md (NASA Kids, National Geographic Kids, BBC Bitesize).
- Experiments: only safe household items, no heat/flames/sharp tools/chemicals, no tasting;
  always with a grown-up.
- Numbers rounded kid-friendly but true ("the Sun is very, very far — more than 100 of our
  Earths could fit across it").

## Visual devices

Space / Underwater / Meadow sets · diagram arrows (stage-space SVG, thick, animated draw-on) ·

## Formats

Short (9:16, 45–60 s): one question, one model. Long (16:9, 4–7 min): one topic, 3 connected
questions (the water cycle: evaporation, clouds, rain) with a recap song.
