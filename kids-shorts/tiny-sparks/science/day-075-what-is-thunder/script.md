**Sources:** NOAA/NWS JetStream "Thunder" (lightning heats the air to ~30,000 °C in a fraction of a second; the rapid expansion
creates the sound wave we hear as thunder; nearby = a sharp crack, distant = a rumble because sound from different parts of
the channel arrives at different times); NOAA SciJinks (lightning ~5× hotter than the Sun's surface); NWS Lightning Safety
("When thunder roars, go indoors"; thunder can be heard ~10 miles away, and if you can hear it you are close enough to be
struck); National Geographic Kids (all lightning produces thunder). Paper-bag pop: safe with a grown-up (no heat, nothing
small to swallow).
