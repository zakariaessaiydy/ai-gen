WHY? (snow is falling) → GUESS (Leo: a giant polar bear shakes a bag of sugar!) → "frozen water" → Drippy is back →
1 how does Drippy become snow? (up into a winter cloud, colder than a freezer; a speck of dust; FREEZE → the one word ICE
CRYSTAL, your turn) · 2 how does a snowflake grow? (it grows arms one by one — count to SIX, show six fingers) · 3 why
snow and not rain? (cold air all the way down; warm air near the ground → rain) → Drippy lands on a mitten → millions of
snowflakes → WOW: almost every snowflake looks a little different (and so are you) → MAKE IT with a grown-up: a paper
snowflake (fold, fold, a grown-up cuts, open: six arms) → build a snowman → in the sun it melts (back to the water cycle)
→ remember (5 questions) → chant → bye.

Sources: NOAA/NWS "How do snowflakes form?" (supercooled droplets freeze on dust/pollen, crystals grow by vapour
deposition; ice's hexagonal structure → six-sided snowflakes) · Met Office (snow reaches the ground when the air stays
cold enough) · "no two alike" phrased as "almost every snowflake looks a little bit different" (Libbrecht, Caltech).
