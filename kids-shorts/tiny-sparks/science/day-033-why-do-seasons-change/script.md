Facts (true, simplified, not falsified):
1. Seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis (~23.5°) as Earth orbits the Sun, not by distance — Earth is
   actually closest to the Sun in early January (northern winter). (NASA Space Place — "What causes the seasons?")
2. When a hemisphere tilts toward the Sun, sunlight hits it more directly (more energy per area) and days are
   longer → summer; tilted away → spread-out light, shorter days → winter. (NASA Space Place; BBC Bitesize KS2)
3. The Northern and Southern Hemispheres have opposite seasons. (National Geographic Kids; NASA)
4. One orbit around the Sun takes about 365 days — one year. (NASA)
Experiment: flashlight on paper, straight vs. tilted, with a grown-up — no heat, nothing sharp.
