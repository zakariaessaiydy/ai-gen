WHY? → GUESS ("the Moon has a big night-light inside!" — corrected kindly) → MODEL: the Moon goes dark grey —
it has NO light of its own → the Sun appears → a sunbeam hits the Moon, the Moon lights up, and the light
bounces down to the kids → ONE word: REFLECT (your-turn pause, Leo repeats) → SEE IT (with a grown-up: a
flashlight on a ball in a dark room — the ball "glows") → Leo: "So moonlight is really sunlight!" (confetti)
→ RECAP 3 cards (Sun shines · hits the Moon · bounces to us) → WOW: the Moon is really dark grey, like an old
road → "Keep asking why!".

Facts (true, simplified, not falsified):
1. The Moon does not make its own light; moonlight is sunlight reflected off its surface. (NASA Space Place —
   "Why does the Moon shine?" / NASA Science: Moon)
2. The Moon's surface is dark — it reflects only about 12% of the sunlight, similar to worn asphalt ("dark
   grey, like an old road"). (NASA Science — Moon facts; Sky & Telescope)
Experiment: a flashlight and a ball in a dark room — safe household items, "with a grown-up".
