# Day 26 — Why Do We Have Day and Night? (Short 9:16, 1 min 52 s)

Niche: 🚀 Science for Kids #4 · ages 3–5 · cast (hero outfits): Spark Girl/Mila (asks), Captain Leo (wrong

HOOK: the meadow turns from day to night while Mila asks → GUESS (Leo: "the Sun goes to bed and sleeps all

sleeps · Earth SPINS like a top · top-down diagram: the side facing the Sun = DAY, the side facing away = NIGHT
→ OUR HOUSE rides the spin; a sky window shows what it sees: "Good morning!" as it turns into the light,
"Good night!" as it turns away → YOUR TURN: spin like the Earth (the cast spins) → SEE IT with a grown-up:
flashlight + ball + a sticker that turns into the light, then into the dark → "The Earth spins!" → night for

whole day → sunrise back to the day meadow (loops).
ONE word taught: SPIN. Built with the PACING rule.

Facts (true, simplified, not falsified):
1. Day and night happen because the Earth spins (rotates) on its axis; the half facing the Sun has day, the
   half facing away has night. (NASA Space Place — "What causes day and night?"; BBC Bitesize KS2 — Day and night)
2. The Sun does not go out or move away at night — it keeps shining; it only seems to rise and set because we
   turn. (NASA Space Place)
3. When it is night for us, it is daytime on the opposite side of the world. (National Geographic Kids; BBC Bitesize)
4. One full spin takes about 24 hours — one day. (NASA Space Place)
Experiment: flashlight + ball (+ sticker) in a dark room, with a grown-up — no heat, nothing sharp.
