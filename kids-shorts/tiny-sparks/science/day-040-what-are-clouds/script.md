# Day 40 — What Are Clouds? (SHORT 9:16, 1:43)

Niche: 🚀 Science for Kids #6 · ages 3–5 · cast: Spark Girl (Mila), Captain Leo (wrong guess), Professor Hoot.
WHY? (Mila points at the clouds) → GUESS (Leo: cotton candy! Hoot: good guess… but clouds are WATER) → MODEL by the
sea: the warm Sun shines on the sea → a little water goes up, invisible → the ONE word EVAPORATION (your turn) → up
high it's cold, it turns into tiny drops → millions of drops gather = a cloud → big heavy drops fall = rain (splash)
→ SEE IT with a grown-up: breathe on a cold mirror = a tiny cloud of drops → RECAP 4 icons → WOW fact → bye.

Sources:
- Water cycle / clouds = tiny water droplets or ice crystals formed when water vapour cools (NASA Climate Kids,
  "What are clouds?"; National Geographic Kids).
- Breath fogging a cold mirror = water vapour condensing into droplets (same process, safe home demo).
- Cloud weight: a typical cumulus holds ~500 metric tons of water (USGS Water Science School, ~1.1 million lb);
  an African elephant is ~6 t → "as much as about eighty elephants".
