# Day 61 — How Do Rainbows Appear? (LONG 16:9, 5 min 18 s)

Niche: 🚀 Science for Kids #9 · ages 3–6 · cast: Spark Girl + Captain Leo + Prof. Hoot + Drippy the raindrop (Days 47,
54) + narrator · places: meadow after the rain · a sky model · science-lab (painted) · energy layer standard.

WHY? (a rainbow draws itself over the wet meadow) → GUESS (Leo: a giant painter with a huge paintbrush!) → Hoot: it's
sunlight + raindrops → Drippy is back → 1 WHAT IS SUNLIGHT MADE OF? (white light through a prism fans into 7 colours —
say them; show 7 fingers; colours all around us: apple, orange, banana, leaf, sky, night sky, flower) → 2 HOW DOES A
RAINDROP MAKE A RAINBOW? (light goes in, bends and bounces, the colours spread out; millions of drops = one big rainbow)
→ 3 WHERE DO WE LOOK? (sun behind you, rain in front — think-timer game) → WOW: a rainbow is really a circle (seen from a
plane) → double rainbow (colours upside down) → no sun, no rainbow → MAKE IT with a grown-up (a glass of water in a sunny
window, white paper → a little rainbow) → colour games (after yellow? on top?) → remember (5) → chant → bye.

Facts: refraction + internal reflection of sunlight in raindrops, sun behind the viewer (Met Office / NOAA); the whole
bow is a circle (visible from aircraft); the secondary bow's colours are reversed; red is on the outside of the primary bow.
